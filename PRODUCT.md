# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Developers and AI coding agents working on applications that may become real businesses or operational systems. They need a repository whose intent, boundaries, failure behavior, and unresolved production responsibilities are understandable without reverse engineering the codebase.

## Product Purpose

Aruo installs an explicit AI engineering contract into a repository, creates projects from intentional foundations, and audits locally detectable production concerns. Success means a developer can understand what is expected, an agent can modify the repository without guessing its rules, and missing production work is visible. Aruo never claims that a contract or scaffold alone makes an application production-ready.

## Brand Personality

Intentional, candid, dependable. The product should create confidence through explicit contracts and working evidence, not through impressive-looking feature lists.

## Anti-references

- Generic SaaS dashboards, fake analytics, billing screens, task trackers, workspaces, and other invented product domains.
- Boilerplates that install many integrations without exercising or explaining them.
- Decorative terminal output presented as evidence of engineering quality.
- “Production-ready” claims based on file presence, badges, or happy-path builds.
- Clever abstraction layers that hide native framework behavior.

## Design Principles

1. Intent is first-class: managed rules state when they apply, and completion claims distinguish repository evidence from unverified operational knowledge.
2. Keep product decisions out of infrastructure: provide capabilities and seams, not fictional business models.
3. Make failure legible: errors explain what failed, the effect, and the next useful action.
4. Prefer native conventions and boring infrastructure over clever cross-platform abstractions.
5. Evidence outranks claims: `aruo doctor` reports what can be demonstrated and labels unresolved concerns honestly.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

The CLI supports keyboard operation, no-color and ASCII environments, redirected streams, narrow terminals, and Huh's accessible prompt mode. Color, Unicode, and animation are enhancements rather than information carriers.
