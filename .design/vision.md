# Working vision

Aruo should make excellent repository engineering ordinary, explainable, and durable.

In five years, a maintainer should be able to adopt an unfamiliar repository, understand its contracts and risks, compare it with an explicit standard, plan an upgrade, verify the result, prepare a release, and onboard a contributor through one coherent local model. Organizations may coordinate the same model across fleets without making the hosted layer mandatory.

## Desired future

- Repository intent is declarative and versioned.
- Quality claims link to reproducible evidence.
- Standards evolve through previewable migrations rather than copy-paste campaigns.
- Generated projects retain native identity and user ownership.
- Plugins extend capabilities without inheriting unrestricted trust.
- Documentation and examples remain executable product surfaces.
- Architecture assistance cites repository facts and accepted decisions.

## Boundaries

Aruo must not become a universal build system, opaque health score, autonomous publisher, mandatory cloud account, unreviewed plugin marketplace, or collection of abandoned templates. It should not hide organizational policy behind “best practice” language or imply production readiness from generated files.

## Unresolved vision questions

- How much organization policy belongs locally versus in an optional service?
- Can repository health be summarized without encouraging metric gaming?
- Where should deterministic migration stop and human architectural judgment begin?
- Which forms of assistance remain useful when AI is unavailable or prohibited?
