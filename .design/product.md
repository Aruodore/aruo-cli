# Product thinking

## Core hypothesis

Developers do not primarily need another way to copy starter files. They need continuity between repository intent, observed state, engineering standards, safe change, and evidence over the full project lifecycle.

## Primary users

- Open-source maintainers who want strong defaults without permanent platform dependence.
- Contributors who need an understandable route from clone to valid change.
- Platform teams that need consistent outcomes across native language ecosystems.
- Research and performance teams that need reproducible evidence rather than claims.

## Product promise

Aruo should help a maintainer answer:

1. What does this repository intend to be?
2. What is actually present and working?
3. What evidence supports its quality claims?
4. What changed from policy or blueprint history?
5. What is the safest next action?
6. How can that action be previewed, reversed, and explained?

## Positioning

Aruo is a local-first repository lifecycle control plane. Creation is an entry point, not the product boundary. Native tools remain authoritative; Aruo coordinates intent, plans, policy, provenance, and evidence around them.

## Product tensions to preserve

- Opinionated defaults versus ecosystem autonomy.
- Powerful automation versus user ownership and safety.
- Cross-language consistency versus native conventions.
- Immediate usefulness versus ten-year maintainability.
- Rich evidence versus fast feedback and low noise.
- Extensibility versus workstation supply-chain risk.

## Anti-metrics

Do not optimize for templates published, checks counted, repositories created, plugin installs, terminal output volume, or AI-generated changes. Prefer safe upgrade rate, finding usefulness, onboarding completion, release reliability, local/CI parity, and maintained-project retention.
