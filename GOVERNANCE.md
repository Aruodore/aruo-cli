# Governance

## Principles

Decision making is transparent, evidence-driven, conflict-aware, and proportional to reversibility. Authority follows sustained responsibility, not employment or volume of contributions.

## Roles

- **Contributor:** anyone making a constructive contribution.
- **Reviewer:** trusted domain contributor who can approve within defined ownership.
- **Maintainer:** steward with merge, release, triage, security, and governance duties.
- **Lead maintainer:** coordinates cross-cutting decisions and resolves documented stalemates.
- **Security responders:** limited group handling embargoed reports; membership reviewed regularly.

Role changes are proposed publicly by maintainers, consider quality, judgment, collaboration, and sustained availability, and are recorded in `MAINTAINERS.md` before community launch. Access is least-privilege and removed promptly when duties end.

## Decisions

Routine changes use PR review and CODEOWNERS. Significant proposals use RFCs; durable architectural choices use ADRs. Consensus is preferred. If unresolved after the stated review period, maintainers document positions and the lead maintainer decides; affected parties may request one reconsideration with new evidence. Conflicted decision makers disclose and recuse.

Two authorized maintainers approve releases and high-impact security/protocol/governance changes where staffing permits. Emergency security changes may be handled privately, then documented after disclosure.

## Responsibilities

Maintainers review fairly, preserve compatibility, protect credentials and reporters, uphold conduct, maintain releases/docs/CI, mentor contributors, declare conflicts, and plan succession. Maintainer status may become emeritus after sustained inactivity and can be restored by mutual agreement.

## Long-term stewardship

The project will define trademark ownership, fiscal/legal home, domain/package ownership, and succession before 1.0. Governance changes require an RFC and supermajority of active maintainers; no single vendor may unilaterally close the open core, schemas, or conformance suite.
